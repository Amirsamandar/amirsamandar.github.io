summary: >-
  Shows that the KL divergence, the standard forecast of whether a model is detectable, does not by itself give the probability of detecting it.
detail: >-
  For multivariate Gaussian data, derives the distribution of the log-likelihood ratio and its cumulants under both hypotheses, and examines discovery potential and parameter-estimation accuracy when the null is a point and the alternative a continuous family. Case studies: a toy Gaussian model, the cubic three-torus, the tensor-to-scalar ratio from B-mode polarization, and dark energy from supernova distances. Detectability also depends on the size of the parameter space, the likelihood-ratio distributions, and the distance from parameter-space boundaries (the look-elsewhere effect), so the KL–detectability relation has to be calibrated for each model family and test statistic.
contribution: >-

why: >-
  Lets detectability forecasts be calibrated for the model family and test statistic actually used, rather than read off the KL divergence alone.
